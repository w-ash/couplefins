# v1.11.x: Settlement Coverage

For strategic overview, see the [planning overview](README.md).

## Why this version

The running FIFO ledger (v1.7.5) applied every payment to the oldest open
month, treating the `year`/`month` annotation as display only. When Settle Up
became year-scoped (v1.10.0), that broke visibly: three $1,981 payments meant
for 2026 rent were applied to 2025, so the 2026 view showed gross charges with
no evidence the payments existed — "Ash owes Kew $3,984.30" where the correct
figure is "Kew owes Ash $1,958.70". The math was also implemented twice
(`src/domain/ledger.py` and `web/src/lib/ledger.ts`) and the copies drifted.

This version replaces FIFO application with **explicit coverage**: every
settlement records the months it covers as a list of **(year, month, amount)
portions** summing to the settlement amount — one mechanism, as low-surface-area
as possible. Portions are allocated at record time in Python and stored; month
balance = charges' shares net − portions allocated to it; year = sum of its
months; display math is purely additive with no reallocation ever. All
settle-up math moves to Python; the UI renders precomputed, direction-resolved
numbers.

### v1.11.0: Settlement coverage — per-month portions, all math in Python
**Goal**: Every number on Settle Up belongs to one calendar year, reflects the payments made, is computed in Python, and no two surfaces can disagree.
**Status**: Completed (2026-08-24)
**Context**: Verified production numbers pin the model. Each monthly rent payment is, in real life, three transactions — the charge (Kew's Check −$3,962.00, split 50/50) plus both Venmo transfer legs (Ash −$1,981 out, Kew +$1,981 in — exactly Ash's half). In the system each is a settlement with both legs linked and one portion: $1,981 covering its rent month. With rent covered, each month's residual swings toward Ash — the normal state:

| Period | Charges (net) | Rent portion | Must display |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan 2026 | 1,956.89 | 1,981.00 | Kew owes Ash $24.11 |
| Feb 2026 | 222.31 | 1,981.00 | Kew owes Ash $1,758.69 |
| Mar 2026 | 1,805.10 | 1,981.00 | Kew owes Ash $175.90 |
| **2026** | **3,984.30** | **5,943.00** | **Kew owes Ash $1,958.70** |

**What this unlocks**: Payments land where the couple meant them; a planned Jan–Mar catch-up lump (portioned across those three months) brings the residuals to zero.

#### Post-Deploy Revisions

- [x] **Settlement direction derived from linked transfer legs**
    - Effort: S
    - What: A linked settlement's `from`/`to` is derived from its legs, not stated: the negative leg's payer is the sender, the positive leg's payer the recipient, a single leg fills the other side from the couple, and contradictory legs are rejected (`derive_direction_from_legs` in `src/application/use_cases/_shared/settlement_records.py`). `RecordSettlementUseCase` ignores the command's direction when legs are present; post-hoc linking (`MarkTransactionAsSettlementUseCase`) rejects a leg contradicting the stored direction via `assert_legs_match_direction`; the chat propose card corrects the model's stated direction from the fetched legs; `LinkSettlementSection` computes sender/recipient from the selected candidates (`deriveSettlementDirection` in `web/src/components/CandidateChecklist.tsx`) instead of the outstanding-balance direction. One prod row repaired: the July 2026 rent settlement was stored Kew → Ash while its own Venmo legs showed Ash −$1,981 / Kew +$1,981 — swapped to Ash → Kew; audit after: zero mismatches across all 7 linked settlements.
    - Why: The link flow stamped the settlement with the selected year's outstanding-balance direction. That was latently wrong and became visible the first time the balance flipped (recording Apr–Jun rent flipped 2026 to "Kew owes Ash", so July's recording inherited the reversed direction and swung its summary by $3,962). Deriving from the legs makes the wrong-direction state unrepresentable through every linking door.
    - Dependencies: None
    - Status: Completed (2026-08-24)

- [x] **Signed portions — a payment settles the net of the months it covers**
    - Effort: M
    - What: `plan_portions` no longer sweeps covered months oldest-first clearing gross balances and skipping the ones owed *to* the payer. It brings every covered month to zero — positive portion where the payer owes, negative where the month is owed to them — with any surplus landing on the newest covered month; portions stay summing to the settlement amount, now as signed values. A payment short of its covered span pays the oldest months first out of what is available, leaving the newest partly or wholly unpaid rather than swinging one against the payer. `SettlementPortion.amount` accepts a negative amount and rejects only zero; month status reads portion membership rather than a `paid` sum that opposite-direction portions can cancel to zero; `portionsLabel` renders a negative portion as `$393.64 ← Jun`. No migration — the column is a string and existing portions are all positive.
    - Why: A settlement's portions all carried the settlement's direction and had to be positive, so one payment could never zero a covered span whose months ran both ways — and by the time rent is settled monthly, a span routinely does. Sizing the payment to the span's net (the only sensible number to send) therefore guaranteed it ran out early. On production a Kew → Ash lump of $4,715.23 covering Jan–Aug 2026 — exactly the net of Jan–Jul — cleared Jan–Mar, dumped its remainder on April and left May, June and July untouched.
    - Dependencies: v1.11.0 (portion allocation and balance math)
    - Status: Completed (2026-09-06)
    - Notes: Regression fixture `TestMixedDirectionCatchUp` in `tests/unit/domain/test_ledger.py` models the production case — the seven pre-lump residuals running both ways and the seven signed portions. The v1.11.0 acceptance table passes unchanged. Prod repair (pending, done from the UI): delete the miscast lump and re-record it covering Jan–Jul. Dry-run against the production rows gives portions 24.11 / 1758.69 / 192.40 / 2755.15 / −6.05 / −393.67 / 384.60, summing to 4715.23 exactly — Jan–Jul all settle and the 2026 total stays "Ash owes Kew $315.64".
