# v0.9.x: Transaction Classification & Budget Scope

For strategic overview, see the [planning overview](README.md).

**Design basis**: The current system uses `payer_percentage: int | None` where `None` means personal and any value means shared. This conflates "the split ratio" with "is this relevant to the couple?" and can't represent two real scenarios:

- **Spotted**: One person fronts money for the other's expense (their subscription, parking ticket). Needs 100% reimbursement at settlement.
- **Household (no split)**: An expense relevant to the couple's shared life but paid individually — concert tickets bought separately for a show you attend together. No settlement impact, but should count toward the shared budget.

The fix: two orthogonal fields on each transaction, plus per-category budget scope.

| Type | `household` | `payer_percentage` | Settlement? | Budget? | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Personal | false | 100 | No | Only if category has `include_personal` | (default) |
| Shared | true | 1-99 | Yes (split) | Yes | `shared` / `split` tag |
| Spotted | true | 0 | Yes (100% reimbursement) | Yes | other person's name as tag |
| Household (no split) | true | 100 | No | Yes | `household` tag, or `shared, s100` |

#### Settlement vs. budget: two independent questions

Every transaction answers two independent questions:

1. **"Does someone owe money?"** → settlement. Determined by `payer_percentage < 100`. The `household` flag is irrelevant.
2. **"Does this count toward our shared budget?"** → budget. Determined by `household=true`, or the category has `include_personal=true`.

These are orthogonal. A transaction can affect settlement without affecting budget, affect budget without affecting settlement, affect both, or affect neither.

#### Worked examples

**Alice and Bob go out to dinner together. Alice pays the $80 bill.**
Tags: `shared`. Fields: `household=true`, `payer_percentage=50`.
→ Settlement: Yes. Bob owes Alice $40.
→ Budget: Yes. $80 counts toward Food & Dining.

**Alice buys Bob's $15 prescription. Bob forgot their wallet.**
Tags: `bob`. Fields: `household=true`, `payer_percentage=0`.
→ Settlement: Yes. Bob owes Alice the full $15.
→ Budget: Yes. $15 counts toward Health & Wellness.

**Alice and Bob each buy their own $60 concert ticket for a show they attend together. Alice tags theirs:**
Tags: `household`. Fields: `household=true`, `payer_percentage=100`.
→ Settlement: No. Alice paid their own way — nobody owes anyone.
→ Budget: Yes. Alice's $60 counts toward Lifestyle. (So does Bob's, if they also tagged theirs.)

**Alice grabs a $5 coffee on the way to work. No tags.**
Tags: (none). Fields: `household=false`, `payer_percentage=100`.
→ Settlement: No.
→ Budget: No — unless "Coffee Shops" has `include_personal=true`, in which case it counts toward Food & Dining.

**Alice buys $120 in groceries, mostly household staples. They tag it shared but cover 70%.**
Tags: `shared, s70`. Fields: `household=true`, `payer_percentage=70`.
→ Settlement: Yes. Bob owes Alice $36 (30% of $120).
→ Budget: Yes. The full $120 counts toward Food & Dining.

**Bob buys a $40 bag of personal supplements. No tags, but "Supplements" has `include_personal=true`.**
Tags: (none). Fields: `household=false`, `payer_percentage=100`.
→ Settlement: No.
→ Budget: Yes — because the category opts in to personal spending. The $40 counts toward Health & Wellness even though it's not a household expense.

### Why this design (and what we rejected)

**Why "spotted" (not "paid-for-you")**: The original spec used "paid-for-you" but the second person ("you") has no clear referent in a two-person system. Alternatives considered: "covered" (implies not expected to be paid back), "reimbursable" (sounds like business expenses), "fronted" (close, but "spotted" is more natural — "I spotted Bob for lunch").

**Why two fields (not one or three)**: An earlier design used three orthogonal fields — `is_shared: bool`, `beneficiary_person_id: UUID | None`, and `payer_percentage: int | None`. We simplified `beneficiary_person_id` away (with two people, `payer_percentage=0` implies the other person) and made `payer_percentage` non-nullable. But a single field can't distinguish "my solo coffee" from "concert we attended together but paid separately" — both are `payer_percentage=100`. Budget relevance is a per-transaction decision, so `household` stays as an explicit flag.

**Why `household` is per-transaction (not per-category-group)**: A per-group toggle would be too blunt — it would include ALL personal spending in that group, even solo activities. The couple needs per-transaction control: this specific concert was a shared experience, that one I went to alone. The `household` tag (or `shared` tag) in Monarch provides that signal.

**Why `include_personal` is per-category (not per-category-group)**: Within the same group (e.g., Food & Dining), the couple might want to track all grocery spending (personal and household) but only shared dining. Per-category granularity lets them set "Groceries: include personal" while leaving "Dining Out: household only." Categories are promoted to first-class entities to support this.

**Why `is_shared` → `household` rename**: The `shared` tag in Monarch sets `household=true`, but so does the new `household` tag (which doesn't imply a 50/50 split). The field's meaning expanded: it no longer means "this expense is split" — it means "this is relevant to the couple's shared life." `household` better captures that broader semantics. The `is_shared` DB column is renamed, not dropped.

**Why "household" (not "pooled" or "combined")**: Research into couples budgeting apps (YNAB, Honeydue, Goodbudget, Monarch) found that most default to household-scoped budgets — all spending counts regardless of who paid. "Household" is the established personal finance term. "Pooled" connotes combining income into shared accounts (a money management structure, not a budget scope). "Combined" is vague.

**The budget insight**: When shared meals go down one month, it might be because personal meals went up — total spending is the same, just the split changed. The couple might buy concert tickets together sometimes and separately other times, but it's all entertainment spending. How a thing was paid for shouldn't affect how it shows up in the shared budget.

**`sXX` is authoritative**: If present, `sXX` sets `payer_percentage` regardless of other tags. If multiple `sXX` tags exist, the highest value wins (payer takes the most share). This gives one unambiguous source of truth for the split.

### v0.9.0: Split Continuum + Household Flag + Spotted Detection
**Goal**: Make `payer_percentage` non-nullable (always 0-100). Rename `is_shared` → `household`. Add `household` tag recognition. Detect person-name tags during CSV import. Settlement uses the split; budget uses the household flag.
**Context**: The couple already tags spotted transactions with the other person's name in Monarch (e.g., Alice tags Bob's expenses as `bob`). The `is_shared` column already exists in the DB — it's renamed to `household` with expanded semantics (the new `household` Monarch tag sets it without implying a 50/50 split). An earlier design had three fields but we simplified to two orthogonal fields — see "Why this design" above.
**What this unlocks**: Spotted transactions enter settlement at 100% reimbursement. Household-but-not-split transactions (concert you attended together, paid separately) appear in budgets without affecting settlement.

### v0.9.1: Category Entity + Budget Scope
**Goal**: Promote categories from bare strings to first-class entities. Add `include_personal: bool` per category to widen budget scope beyond household transactions.
**Context**: The `household` flag (v0.9.0) controls which transactions are "relevant to the couple." But some categories — like Groceries — the couple wants to track ALL spending, even personal. Per-category granularity is needed because within the same group (Food & Dining), Groceries might include personal spending while Dining Out does not. Most couples budgeting apps (YNAB, Honeydue, Goodbudget) default to household-scoped budgets; Couplefins needs a toggle because it starts from reconciliation rather than budgeting.
**What this unlocks**: Holistic spending visibility for specific categories where individual and shared spending are part of the same budget. "When shared meals go down, it might be because personal meals went up."

This replaces the bare-string category model. CategoryMapping absorbed — the category entity now owns its group assignment. Add `CategoryRepositoryProtocol`.
    - Why: Categories need configurable properties. `include_personal` is the first, but having a proper entity enables future per-category config without further schema changes.

### v0.9.3: Transaction Exclusion
**Goal**: Flag individual transactions to exclude from reconciliation without deleting them.
**Context**: Sometimes a transaction is tagged `shared` in Monarch but shouldn't be included in reconciliation. `is_excluded` is a simple override: excluded transactions are excluded from BOTH settlement and budget, regardless of `payer_percentage` or `household`.
**What this unlocks**: Quick "this one's not shared" corrections without editing tags or re-uploading.

    - Why: One-line domain change with high UX value
