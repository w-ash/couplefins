# v1.15.x: Co-owned Home

For strategic overview, see the [planning overview](README.md).

## Why this version

The maintainer (2026-10-12): this is the next thing built. Two people who co-own a home pay its
costs from joint bank accounts, and couplefins becomes where those payments are logged, with a
spreadsheet backup through Read API v1 (unscheduled, External read interface). It also tracks
each owner's share in the home as it grows, any transaction can be tagged as counting toward a
share, and the home gets a page of its own (House page). These stories sketch the shape of that
work; they are not a spec.

The versions below split the work in dependency order, except that the House page comes
first on dummy data (v1.15.1), at the maintainer's request. Re-cut them when the stories are
revised.

**Before building anything in this series**

- Learn the owners' actual financial setup for the home first, from the maintainer and from the
  maintainer's private notes on it (ask where they are; nothing from them comes into this repo):
  the accounts, the loans and where each stands, the co-ownership agreement as signed, the
  decisions about who pays what, and what is already logged elsewhere.
- Don't assume these stories capture every requirement, or the situation as it stands when the
  work starts. They were written before the agreement was signed, while the purchase was financed
  by short-term loans awaiting a refinance, so both will have moved on.
- Revise the stories with the maintainer, then build. Where the setup and a story disagree, the
  setup wins.
- Keep the model generic, a co-owned asset with a capital account per owner, so it works for any
  couple. The household's rules, names, and figures are data entered in the app, never code,
  docs, fixtures, or commit messages (CLAUDE.md: public repo). Examples use Alice and Bob.

**The setup as understood on 2026-10-12** (confirm every point)

- Two joint accounts: checking, which pays the bills and the renovation (each owner has a debit
  card), and savings, whose named buckets set money aside (property tax, insurance, a repair
  reserve, renovation money not yet spent).
- Each owner deposits into checking from their own bank. Money given to one owner belongs to
  that owner wherever it sits, and some of it pays house costs.
- The co-ownership agreement sets each owner's share as their qualifying contributions divided
  by both owners' totals, dollar for dollar, with no interest or timing adjustment.
    - Qualifying: down payment and closing costs; interest and principal on loans both owners
      approved; property tax; insurance; necessary repairs above a threshold; other work only when
      both owners agreed its scope and cost split before it started. Nothing else counts unless
      both agree.
    - Not qualifying: utilities (split, but credited to no one), rent, and moving costs.
    - Each bill splits 50/50 by default. Either owner may pay more of a bill when both agree, and
      the extra counts toward the payer. An owner who covers the other's missed payment is
      credited with it.
    - On a sale or buyout, what is left after costs and loans splits by share.
- Money sitting in the accounts (the float, the set-asides, the reserve) is not a contribution
  until it pays a qualifying cost, and on a sale each owner gets back their unspent deposits. This
  was a proposed rule; check whether the signed agreement has it.
- Some qualifying payments never pass through the joint accounts: the closing, through escrow; a
  loan paydown wired from one owner's own account; a premium paid before the accounts opened; a
  house cost on a personal card.
- The owners cap how much of their own money goes into the renovation, apart from gifts and
  loans, so a contribution's source matters as well as its owner.
- Undecided at writing: whether refunds (a returned deposit, a rebate, an insurance refund) and
  payments a lender returns reduce credits, and how a loan settled for less than its face amount
  counts.

### v1.15.0: Accounts and the Importer interface
**Goal**: Every transaction belongs to an account, and every import goes through one `Importer` interface.
**Status**: Completed (2026-10-12)

- [x] **Importers behind one interface**
    - Effort: M
    - What: Every import goes through one `Importer` protocol that turns a file into
      transactions for one uploader. Monarch CSV is the first importer; a bank statement CSV
      (v1.15.2) or a direct feed is a later one.
    - Why: `upload_csv.py` called `parse_monarch_csv` directly, so couplefins could only learn
      what one person's Monarch saw, and Monarch may not stay the source.
    - Dependencies: None
    - Status: Completed (2026-10-12)
    - Notes:
        - `src/domain/parsing/importer.py`: the `Importer` protocol (`name`, `parse(data: bytes,
          ctx: ImportContext) -> ParseResult`). `MonarchCsvImporter` in
          `src/domain/parsing/monarch_csv.py` decodes the file and calls `parse_monarch_csv`,
          whose tag rules (`_classify`) are unchanged. The use cases take the importer by
          constructor injection and share one `parse_file` step.
        - `Upload` records `importer` and `content_sha256`, for provenance. A file imported
          twice is not refused: rows are matched on the natural key, so the second import adds
          nothing, and changes declined from a file can still be accepted later from the same
          file. The maintainer (2026-10-12) chose this over refusing a file whose hash an
          earlier upload has.
        - Acceptance (met): a Monarch CSV imports with results identical to before; the same
          file imported twice adds nothing the second time; an export overlapping the last one
          adds only its new rows.

- [x] **Accounts**
    - Effort: M
    - What: An account is a first-class entity, personal (one owner) or joint (no single
      owner). Every transaction has an `account_id`.
    - Why: A joint account's rows need an account to belong to that is not one person's upload.
    - Dependencies: None
    - Status: Completed (2026-10-12)
    - Notes:
        - Table `accounts (id, name, kind, owner_person_id)`, unique per owner and name; a check
          constraint holds "owner set iff personal". Migration 0015 creates one personal account
          per uploader per Monarch `Account` label and points every row at it, so no figure
          moves.
        - A personal account's id derives from its owner and label
          (`personal_account_id`, uuid5), so an import names the account without a lookup and
          adds any that are missing with one insert that skips existing rows. A joint account
          (v1.15.2) will need its own id rule.
        - The `account` string stays on the transaction as the source's label and stays part of
          the natural key.
        - Acceptance (met): each existing row belongs to a personal account owned by its payer;
          Monarch figures on every page are unchanged.

- [x] **Jointly owned accounts**
    - Effort: L
    - What: An account is a first-class couplefins entity, personal (one owner) or joint. A joint
      account's transactions are imported straight into it, belong to no person's upload, never
      enter settlement, and carry per-owner shares set by rule or by hand.
    - Why: Today a transaction exists only as a row in one person's upload, with that person as
      `payer_person_id`. A payment from a joint account has no such payer: forcing one makes it
      that person's personal spending, or, split, a debt the other owes, though both funded the
      account.
    - Dependencies: Accounts; the source decision above

    - Notes:
        - Migration 0016 swaps the natural key (`uq_transactions_natural_key`, `src/domain/dedup.py`)

          original statement, occurrence). No row changes identity: personal account ids already
          encode owner and label.
        - `owner_shares(tx, person_ids)` and `share_amounts` in `src/domain/splits.py` are the one
          path to per-person shares (first owner rounds half-up, second takes the remainder). A
          joint deposit with a depositor is 100/0 to the depositor, over any owner shares.
          `is_joint_row` and `payer_of` replace direct `payer_person_id` reads. Listed rows get

          shares and a warning, not a 500.

          (`src/domain/joint.py`). Hand-set `depositor_person_id` and `owner_shares` survive
          re-imports through `_IN_APP_KEYS` (the v1.7.1 mechanism).
        - Re-import: a file removes only joint rows its uploader's own uploads imported, so a
          partner's export never deletes the other's rows.
        - Mark/unmark: marking re-keys both partners' existing rows under the label (payer cleared,
          50/50, out of settlement, hand-set split carried into owner shares, natural-key twins
          collapsed keeping the older upload's row; in-app edits on the dropped twin are lost);
          unmarking reverses by upload provenance, restoring `household=true` and a carried split.
          Both refuse on a finalized affected month.
        - Pre-deploy check, on production before tagging; the result must be empty:
          `SELECT date, amount, account_id, original_statement, occurrence, count(*) FROM transactions GROUP BY 1,2,3,4,5 HAVING count(*) > 1;`
        - Which joint rows count as spending stays the maintainer's call via `is_excluded` and
          transfer-kind groups.
        - Deferred: tag or category share rules (a split by rule, then account default, then row
          override).

### v1.15.3: Sources, pots, and labels
**Goal**: Every joint-account row traces to whose money paid it and the pot it came from.
**Status**: Completed (2026-10-15)

- [x] **Namespaced tags for consumer labels**
    - Effort: S
    - What: Tags of the form `<namespace>:<value>` (for example `line:mortgage`) are labels for
      consumers: never treated as split, reserved, or person-name tags, kept across re-imports,
      and filterable with `tag_prefix`.
    - Why: A joint account's log needs each row's budget line. A generic label lets any consumer
      group rows without couplefins knowing what a line is.
    - Dependencies: None
    - Status: Completed (2026-10-15)
    - Notes:
        - `src/domain/tags.py` holds the namespaced-tag regex
          (`[a-z][a-z0-9-]*:[a-z0-9][a-z0-9 ._-]*`: a value may hold letters, digits, spaces,
          `.`, `_`, and `-`). No such tag matches the split pattern, a reserved tag, or a
          person's name, so `line:mortgage` beside `s50` leaves the split at 50.
        - Preservation differs from v1.7.1's `_IN_APP_KEYS`, which keeps a whole field's stored
          value and so would also keep stale non-label tags. `merge_namespaced_tags` instead
          merges the stored row's labels into the incoming row's tags, called from
          `dedup.classify_transactions` before the diff. The preview therefore never proposes
          removing a label, while ordinary tags still follow the source.
        - An import never removes a label, whether it came from Monarch or the app. A label is
          removed or renamed in the app's tag editor.
        - `tag_prefix` filters `GET /reconciliation` and the chat `search_transactions` tool
          (no UI filter yet). `TagFilter` in `transaction_reads.py` validates the prefix once (a
          bad one is a 422, or a tool error in chat). The GIN index on `tags` backs containment
          only, and a month window holds ~100-200 rows, so the prefix check is a deliberate
          in-memory step in the single read module.
        - The ext API check (`GET /api/ext/v1/transactions?tag_prefix=line:`) still lands with
          Read API v1, which is unscheduled.

- [x] **Funding sources and pots**
    - Effort: M
    - What: Each deposit into a joint account records whose money it is and its source (the
      owner's own money, or money given to that owner), and the money in the joint accounts is
      tracked by pot (bills, set-asides, renovation), so each payment traces to the money that
      paid it.
    - Why: A share credit goes to the owner whose money paid; an owner's cap on their own money
      leaves gifts out; unspent deposits go back to their depositor on a sale; and a bank's
      statement export may not show bucket balances.
    - Dependencies: Jointly owned accounts
    - Status: Completed (2026-10-15)
    - Notes:
        - Landed as namespaced tags with balances computed, never stored, as adjustments are
          ("Why adjustments are computed, not stored", `docs/domain.md`): `src/domain/pots.py`.
        - `pot:<name>` on joint deposits, payments, and pot-to-pot transfer legs; a pot's balance
          is the sum of its rows' signed amounts. Rows without a pot tag fall in an "Unassigned"
          pot. Transfer-kind rows count in pot balances but never as payments.
        - `source:own` / `source:gift` on deposits; own when absent.
        - Per owner: deposits (own vs gift), their share of joint spending payments, and
          unspent = deposits - share of payments. Money in with no depositor in a spending
          category is a refund and nets against payments.
        - An excluded row counts nowhere: in no pot balance, deposit, or payment.
        - House page: once a joint account exists, Set aside is real and no longer marked
          example. It lists the real pots, the unassigned bucket last (flagged `is_unassigned`),
          and a Deposits table of each owner's own and gift deposits, share of payments, and
          unspent deposits. Put in stays a whole example section, "Deposits not yet spent"
          included, until v1.15.4. The Tag Reference guide documents the labels.
        - Deferred to v1.15.4: the bank balance beside each pot (House settings), so the
          match-the-bank difference stays empty. `share:<kind>` is reserved for v1.15.4 and
          `loan:<slug>` for v1.15.5.

### v1.15.4: Owner shares
**Goal**: Each owner's capital account and share percentage, from tagged rows and hand entries.
**Status**: Completed (2026-10-16)

- [x] **Owner shares in a co-owned asset**
    - Effort: L
    - What: Any transaction can be tagged as a qualifying contribution of a given kind; each
      owner's credit is their share of it; couplefins keeps each owner's running capital account
      and share percentage (their total divided by both totals) over time.
    - Why: The maintainer (2026-10-12): couplefins must track each owner's share in the home as
      it grows, and spending that goes to a share must be taggable. The agreement sets the share
      from these credits, and a sale or buyout splits by it.
    - Dependencies: Jointly owned accounts; Funding sources and pots; Namespaced tags for
      consumer labels
    - Status: Completed (2026-10-16)
    - Notes:
        - Computed, never stored, as adjustments are ("Why adjustments are computed, not stored",
          `docs/domain.md`): `src/domain/capital.py` (credits, capital accounts, shares to 0.1
          half-up, last owner = 100 minus the others). Settings are `entities/co_owned_asset.py`,
          table `co_owned_assets` (migration 0017), money columns as String per repo convention;
          one asset per couple. Use cases `save_co_owned_asset`, `record_manual_transaction`,
          `list_manual_transactions`, `delete_manual_transaction`; routes `GET/PUT /house/asset`,
          `GET/POST/DELETE /house/manual-entries`; `HouseResponse.asset`. Every House section
          counts the rows dated through the as-of date.
        - The asset's bank balance (with its date, both or neither) stands beside the Set aside
          total, with the difference bank − total of the pots; no balance is kept per pot. Once
          the asset exists, Set aside is real even before a joint account does, so Put in's
          unspent deposits always match it.
        - `share:<kind>` credits each owner their share of the row (`owner_shares`/`share_amounts`;
          personal `s100` or `household` credits the payer) when the kind qualifies; `repair` only
          at or above the threshold. Credits ignore `is_excluded`, `is_settlement`, household, and
          category kind: the tag is the only lever.
        - Refund assumption: a positive row carrying a share tag reduces the credit. Undecided
          with the owners; pinned by one test.
        - Manual credits: a row in a hand-entered joint-kind account (default "Escrow"), payer
          none, `is_excluded`, with its `share:` tag and document reference, under a synthetic
          upload (importer `manual`) hidden from Upload history. Duplicate manual entries take
          distinct occurrences under the natural-key occurrence rule.
        - The hand-entered account holds only hand-entered rows: its name must not be an imported
          account's label; unmarking and marking it are refused (422); and it is no import
          target, so a Monarch account of the same name imports as the uploader's own. Its
          category, "House contribution", maps on first use to the transfer-kind group when one
          exists, so Settings has nothing unmapped to raise.
        - Chat: read tool `get_house_summary`; the House writes are blacklisted from chat.
        - The agreed-work record (job, agreed split, agreement date) and linking a refund to the
          row it reverses are not built.
        - Owner credits, capital accounts, and percentages reach the Read API as an additive v1
          change, for the spreadsheet backup.
        - Acceptance met (US-HOUSE-10): tagged rows add each owner's share; percentages sum to
          100; untagged, `share:none`, and 50/50 utility rows add nothing; each month's capital
          equals the credits through that month.

    - Effort: M
    - What: Contributions that never pass through an imported account (a closing paid through

    - Why: Some of the largest credits, the down payment and closing costs among them, come

### v1.15.5: Loans and upcoming obligations
**Goal**: The loans and what comes due, each linked to the payment that settles it.
**Status**: Completed (2026-10-17)

- [x] **Loans and upcoming obligations**
    - Effort: M
    - What: The home's loans (lender, principal, rate, term, payment, current balance) and its
      scheduled obligations (property tax installments, a one-off tax bill, insurance renewals,
      loan payments) are recorded with their due dates, amounts, and the bucket or account that
      pays each.
    - Why: The House page shows the mortgage and what is coming due, and neither is in a bank
      statement. The loans also change over time (short-term loans at purchase, a refinance
      later), so a loan is replaced, not edited.
    - Dependencies: Jointly owned accounts
    - Status: Completed (2026-10-17)
    - Notes:
        - Entered by hand. Domain in `src/domain/loans.py` and `src/domain/obligations.py`, with
          `Loan` and `Obligation` entities, use cases, and routes under `/house/loans` and
          `/house/obligations` (migration 0018, tables `loans` and `obligations`).
        - Balance, decided with the maintainer: the statement balance minus the principal part of
          linked payments dated after the statement date. Payments link by `loan:<slug>`. A
          payment also tagged `share:loan-principal` is all principal on either kind. Otherwise
          interest-only is all interest, and amortizing pays interest = balance x rate / 12 to
          the cent first, the rest principal.
        - A loan is replaced at a refinance (old one closes and points to the new) or closed, on
          any date; either deletes its unpaid payment obligations due after the closing date. No
          update-terms use case exists, as the guard; delete is refused once payments,
          obligations, or a replaced loan exist. The one in-place edit is a new statement balance.
        - Obligation status is computed: paid when linked, late past the delinquency date (the
          due date when none), else due. An estimate flag stays until the bill replaces it.
        - Mark-paid candidates are money-out rows scored by closeness to the due date and amount,
          and by the loan's tag; a row already linked to another obligation is left out, and
          linking it or a money-in row is refused. Removing a paying row warns. "Add the next N
          payments" generates a loan's obligations from the first of this month, after its latest
          payment obligation, and skips months that already have one. A pot's shortfall is
          cumulative in due order.
        - Writes are blacklisted from chat; `get_house_summary` reports loans and upcoming.
        - The House page is fully real once an asset exists; the example payload remains only for
          a couple with no asset (v1.15.6).
        - Acceptance met (US-HOUSE-12, US-HOUSE-13): an obligation shows as due, paid, or late; a
          loan's balance matches its statement; replacing a loan keeps the old one's history.
