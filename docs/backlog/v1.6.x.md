# v1.6.x: Settle Up Audit Table + Transactions Buckets

For strategic overview, see the [planning overview](README.md).

## Why this version

**Two related observations from the together-session ritual:**

1. The Settle Up page shows a single net number and a list of recorded payments — but if the partners disagree on the math, there's nowhere to look. Reconciling means jumping to the Transactions page, eyeballing the table, and adding up shares in your head. The page lacks an audit affordance.

2. The Transactions page header showed a flat total but no decomposition. "How much was household? How much did I spot for my partner? How much was personal?" required scrolling through the table or filtering and re-totalling each time.

Both gaps slow down the 15-minute monthly check. v1.6.0 closes them.

### v1.6.0: Settle Up Audit Table + Transactions Buckets
**Goal**: Surface the math behind the settlement balance, and decompose Transactions-page totals into the buckets that match the domain (household / personal / spotted / partner-paid / excluded).
**Status**: Completed (2026-05-02)
**Context**: The settlement number on Settle Up is opaque — a single figure with no breadcrumb. The Transactions page total was a single sum that flattened the household/personal/spotted distinctions the rest of the app cares about. This version adds a payer-split audit ledger to Settle Up (with copy-for-spreadsheet workflow) and bucketed header cards to Transactions, with a new `spotted` scope to make "I fronted cash for my partner" a first-class filter.
**What this unlocks**: Verifiable settlement math for the together session, faster scope decomposition on Transactions, and reusable per-payer + per-(payer × category-group) aggregates that the chat assistant could query later.

## Decisions

**Audit table sits below the hero card, not behind a tab.** Verification is the second-most-important question on Settle Up (after "what's the number?"). A tab would hide it; a separate page would force navigation away from the settlement actions. Inline-but-disclosed strikes the balance — most reads stop at the narrative, but the receipts are one click away.

**Narrative first, ledger second.** A casual reader gets the answer in plain English. A verifier expands the ledger. The progressive disclosure is the entire UX point — this isn't just a stylistic rename of "How this number works".

**Per-payer share semantics differ from `PersonSummary.total_share`.** `PayerSplitSummary.total_share` is the payer's share of bills they fronted (so `paid - share = partner_share`). `PersonSummary.total_share` is the person's share across *all* bills (used for the broader reconcile-then-settle math). Both are correct for their context — the audit table needs row-local arithmetic, the settlement math needs cross-row.

**`spotted` is a scope, not a separate type.** Internally there's no `is_spotted` flag — the predicate is `payer_percentage === 0 AND household === false AND payer_person_id === current_user`. Adding it as a `TransactionScope` value (alongside `household`, `personal`, `all`) keeps the data model orthogonal while giving users a first-class filter for the "I fronted cash for my partner" case.

**Settlement transactions are excluded from split aggregates.** `filter_split_transactions(...)` drops `is_settlement=true` and `is_excluded=true` rows along with `payer_percentage=100` (household-no-split). Settlement rows still appear in the audit table, but as recorded-settlement ledger rows, not split aggregates — they contribute signed Net values directly.

**`Uncategorized` rows sort last in by-category view.** A separate `_group_sort_key` puts `group_id is None` at the bottom; everything else sorts by `-abs(total)`. Keeps the most material categories at the top.

**Two top-level rendering modes only.** "By payer" (one row per partner) and "By category" (rows per `(payer × category-group)`). No three-way split view. Adding category as a slicing dimension covers the most common follow-up question ("which category drove most of the gap?") without a third toggle.
