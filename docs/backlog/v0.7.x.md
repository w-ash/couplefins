# v0.7.x: Spending Insights & Visualization

For strategic overview, see the [planning overview](README.md).

**Research basis**: Before designing this version series, we studied spending visualization patterns across Monarch Money, YNAB, Copilot Money, Lunch Money, and Actual Budget. We also reviewed couples-finance UX research, financial dataviz anti-patterns, and academic research on money conflicts in relationships. Key findings that shaped every decision below:

- **Line charts, not stacked area** — 12 category groups across 12 months is exactly where stacked area becomes unreadable. Lines stay clean.
- **Small multiples** — one sparkline per category group, scannable at a glance without interaction. Beats a single spaghetti chart with toggles.
- **No auto-generated notifications** — data where users go looking for it, not pushed. "You spent 23% more on coffee!" is patronizing.
- **Neutral framing everywhere** — "$847 of $900", never "almost over budget!" Research shows judgment language triggers financial anxiety.
- **Together-session optimized** — category-level totals are safe conversation territory. Transaction-level detail is for solo prep on the Transactions page.
- **Always pair percentages with absolute amounts** — "up 47%" means nothing without the base amount.
- **Y-axes always start at zero** — truncated axes exaggerate small differences by up to 400%.
- **5-7 KPIs max per view** — more causes dashboard fatigue.
- **Dark mode needs its own palette** — bright saturated colors "bleed" on dark backgrounds. Reduce OKLCH chroma 10-20%, test WCAG AA contrast.

### v0.7.0: Spending Trends Foundation
**Goal**: Deliver the Insights page with small-multiple sparkline charts showing per-group monthly spending trends across the year.
**Context**: The app has rich spending data across months but only shows it as tables and numbers. The couple wants to see trends during their together session — which categories are growing, how this month compares to average, and where the money is going. Small multiples (one mini-chart per category group, shared x-axis) are the best pattern for scanning 12 groups across 12 months — each sparkline is self-contained, so the couple can point at the screen and say "look at Food & Dining" without clicking anything.
**What this unlocks**: Data-driven spending conversations beyond "who owes whom this month." Visual trend scanning across all category groups at a glance. Serves US-BUDGET-4 and Journey 4 (Budget Review).
**Key tech choices**: Recharts for charting (~45KB gzip, React-native, built-in `ReferenceLine`, CSS custom property theming). Small multiples layout using a reusable `SparklineCard` component. New `GetSpendingTrendsUseCase` reuses existing `compute_category_breakdowns()` run per-month across the year. New `/insights` route in sidebar between Budget and Upload, with `TrendingUp` lucide icon. All read-only — no new entities, no persistence changes.

### v0.7.1: Comparison Cards, Budget Lines & Settlement Trend
**Goal**: Enrich the Insights page with "this month vs 3-month average" comparison cards, budget reference lines on sparklines, and a settlement balance trend chart.
**Context**: v0.7.0 shows raw spending trends. Research confirms three enrichments that transform the page from "interesting" to "useful": (1) Comparison cards — "is this month unusual?" at a glance — are the single most actionable insight pattern across all finance apps reviewed. (2) Budget dashed lines on the same axis (never dual-axis) show where the couple intended to be. (3) The settlement balance trend is in no other couples finance app — it reveals whether one person consistently ends up paying more, exposing structural split imbalances the couple might want to address.
**What this unlocks**: Three distinct lenses on the same data: "Is this month unusual?" (comparison cards), "Are we on budget?" (reference lines), and "Is our split fair over time?" (settlement trend).
**Key tech choices**: Backend extends `GetSpendingTrendsResult` with comparison, budget, and settlement data — all derived from existing domain functions, no new entities. Frontend: `ComparisonCard` component, Recharts `ReferenceLine` on sparklines, new `SettlementTrendChart` full-width line chart.

### v0.7.2: Year-over-Year, Dark Mode Tuning & Drill-Down
**Goal**: Add year-over-year comparison overlays, tune the chart palette for dark mode accessibility, and add click-to-expand category detail on sparklines.
**Context**: v0.7.1 shows current-year trends with enrichments. Three polish features complete the picture: (1) YoY comparison — "Are we spending more than last year?" — is one of the most requested unbuilt features across finance app forums. (2) Dark mode charts need explicit attention — research shows colors designed for light backgrounds look washed out or illegible on dark ones. (3) Click-to-expand gives category-level drill-down without leaving the page, following the progressive disclosure pattern: sparkline → categories → transactions (on the Transactions page).
**What this unlocks**: "Are we trending up or down compared to last year?" Year-round polished chart experience in both themes. Category-level detail without page navigation.
**Key tech choices**: Backend extends endpoint with optional `comparison_year` parameter — same domain function, different year. Frontend: Recharts `Line` with `strokeDasharray` for comparison overlay. Dark mode palette via reduced OKLCH chroma in `chart-colors.ts`. Expand/collapse pattern from `BudgetPage.tsx` for drill-down.

## Design Rationale

**Why three subversions instead of two**: v0.7.0 is the riskiest (new charting library, new page, new endpoint, new visualization pattern) — ship it, validate it, then enrich. v0.7.1 is the highest-value enrichment (comparison cards are the most actionable, settlement trend is the most novel). v0.7.2 is polish and power-user features that make the page excellent but aren't blocking core value.

**Why Recharts**: Lightweight (~45KB gzip), React-native composable components, built-in `ReferenceLine` for budget lines, `ResponsiveContainer` for resize, CSS custom property compatibility for theming. No D3 dependency overhead. Active maintenance, good TypeScript support.

**Why not a pace line**: Copilot Money's "ideal vs actual spend rate through the current month" is the most praised pattern in the space, but it requires daily granularity within a single month — a different data shape and chart type from small multiples across months. Could be a dashboard sparkline or a v0.7.3 feature, but doesn't fit the year-overview framing of v0.7.0-v0.7.2.

**Why small multiples over toggle filters**: For 12 groups, a single chart with toggle checkboxes becomes spaghetti. Small multiples let the eye scan across groups without interaction. Each sparkline is self-contained — the couple can point at the screen during their together session without clicking anything.

**Why settlement trend is novel**: No existing couples finance app (Splitwise, Honeydue, Monarch) shows net settlement direction over time. Monthly settlement amounts are shown individually, but the trajectory — "Bob has consistently owed Alice more each month since October" — is invisible without this chart.
